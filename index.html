Expedited treatment options apply only to nonpregnant patients 25 or older.

Special situations

Unsatisfactory test results: when results are insufficient for management (e.g. unsatisfactory cytology), repeat the test as soon as convenient and no later than 4 months. HPV tests are not valid, and should be repeated, if the result is invalid, or if it is negative but part of a cotest whose cytology is unsatisfactory. Repeat an unsatisfactory dual stain unless HPV genotype or cytology alone settles the need for colposcopy. A cotest with unsatisfactory cytology that is HPV 16/18-positive goes to colposcopy. Colposcopy after 2 consecutive unsatisfactory tests. (Perkins 2024; G.2; H.2)

Post hysterectomy: after total hysterectomy, do not screen for vaginal cancer unless the prior 25 years included histology showing CIN 2, CIN 3, AIS or cervical cancer, or HSIL, AGC or persistent ASC-H cytology. Vaginal screening is then annual HPV-based testing until 3 consecutive negatives, then every 3 years for 25 years from the diagnosis. (K.4)

Symptomatic patients: these guidelines are for asymptomatic patients. Abnormal uterine or vaginal bleeding or a visibly abnormal cervix needs diagnostic testing (cytology, colposcopy, imaging, cervical, endocervical or endometrial biopsy).

Over 65 with prior abnormalities: manage as for ages 25–65. Stopping surveillance is unacceptable if the patient is in reasonably good health. (K.5)

Prenatal DES exposure: annual cytology until 65 (ASCCP Clinical Consensus 2024).

Not included: the HHS HIV/opportunistic-infection screening algorithms (couldn't be retrieved; immunosuppressed results use ASCCP §K.3), dual-stain risk numbers for extended genotypes, and Alinity m genotype groupings (the extended-genotyping guidance is specific to Onclarity channels).

Decision support only. It does not replace clinical judgment or the full guideline. It covers asymptomatic patients with a cervix.
